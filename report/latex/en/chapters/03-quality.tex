\section{Construction checks and evidence quality}
The recorded structural validator passes for v4. It checks unique IDs, permitted
labels and predicates, valid endpoint types, accepted status, evidence references,
and recomputed support and weights for derived relations. All ten predicates are
present. The recorded Neo4j load reconciles node and relationship counts;
six integrity queries and an idempotent repeated import also pass. These are
release records, not newly executed database checks for this manuscript.

A full-source consistency check compared the retained authorship and reference
objects for all 13,606 matched works, covering 65,469 positions and 341 cached
response hashes. It establishes agreement with the preserved source snapshot.
Structural consistency and source-byte equality cannot establish the correctness
of provider identities or affiliations.

The prospective source-backed automated review judged 193 of 200 uniformly
sampled accepted edges supported (96.50\%; Wilson 95\% interval
92.95--98.29\%), with one incorrect and six unverifiable judgments. Unverifiable
cases count as nonsupport. These are \textbf{AI-assisted assessments}, not human
annotations or independent validation: construction sources were reused,
evaluator independence is unestablished, and the interval covers sampling
variation rather than evaluator error. A later relation-stratified diagnostic
flagged three topic-derived links and one uncertain authorship; it did not
change the frozen graph and is not pooled into the uniform estimate.

Coverage losses in Table~\ref{tab:coverage} affect what can be seen in the
networks. The graph has 199 papers without accepted authorship, and affiliation
evidence is absent for part of the official byline population. Topic coverage
also measures availability rather than correctness. On 50 historical test
papers, embedding labels had micro precision 0.6863, recall 0.3535 and F1 0.4667
against AI-assigned references, compared with phrase-baseline F1 0.2301.
Those exposed references do not provide an independent human topic test.
Accordingly, inferred topic trends and portfolios are exploratory; observed
paper counts and accepted-edge inventories remain directly measurable.
